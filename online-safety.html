Online Safety

Protect yourself from online threats, scams, and fraud. Learn how to stay safe in the digital world.

Stay Alert: Online fraud cases targeting women have increased by 300% in recent years. Learn how to protect yourself below.
Topic 1

Password Safety

DO This

  • Use a password with letters, numbers, and symbols (e.g., Sunita@2024!)
  • Use different passwords for different apps and accounts
  • Change your password every 3–6 months
  • Use fingerprint or face lock on your phone
  • Write passwords down in a private notebook kept at home

DO NOT Do This

  • Never use easy passwords like 123456 or your name or birthday
  • Never share your password with anyone — not even your husband, children, or friends
  • Never save passwords on public or shared computers
  • Never use the same password for your bank and social media
  • Never type passwords when someone can see your screen
Topic 2

Online Fraud & Scams

Know the common tricks fraudsters use so you can protect yourself

Fake Phone Calls (Vishing)

Someone calls pretending to be a bank officer, government official, or police. They ask for your OTP, Aadhaar number, or bank details.

Real banks and government offices NEVER ask for OTP or PIN on phone. Hang up immediately.

Fake SMS & Links (Phishing)

You receive an SMS saying "Your account is blocked, click here" or "You won a prize." The link takes you to a fake website that steals your data.

Never click links in SMS from unknown numbers. Check the sender's number carefully.

Lottery & Prize Scams

"Congratulations! You won ₹50 lakhs in a lucky draw." They ask you to pay a small "processing fee" first. There is no prize — it is a scam.

You cannot win a lottery you did not enter. No real prize requires you to pay first.

Fake Job Offers

Messages offering work-from-home jobs with high salaries. They ask you to pay a "registration fee" or "training fee" upfront.

Legitimate employers never charge fees for jobs. Report such messages.

Fake QR Code Scam

A scammer sends a QR code saying "Scan this to receive ₹500." Scanning it actually sends money FROM your account.

Scanning a QR code is for PAYING, not for receiving money. Never scan unknown QR codes.

Romance / Friendship Scams

A stranger online becomes very friendly and caring, then asks for money for an emergency — illness, travel, business, or family crisis.

Never send money to someone you have only met online, no matter how much you trust them.
Topic 3

Protecting Your Privacy

Protect Your Identity

  • Never share Aadhaar card photos online
  • Do not post your full address, phone number on social media
  • Cover your phone screen when entering PIN in public
  • Log out of apps on shared phones

Phone Security Settings

  • Set a strong screen lock (PIN / fingerprint)
  • Go to Settings → Apps → Permissions and remove unnecessary permissions
  • Turn off location access for apps that don't need it
  • Enable "Find My Device" in case phone is lost

Protect Your Children

  • Do not share children's photos publicly online
  • Never post school name, location, or routine
  • Enable parental controls on phones used by children
  • Teach children never to talk to strangers online

Financial Privacy

  • Never share bank account number or IFSC via WhatsApp
  • Do not save bank details in phone notes or messages
  • Check your bank statement regularly for unknown transactions
  • Set transaction alerts (SMS) on your bank account
Topic 4

Cyber Harassment & How to Fight Back

What is Cyber Harassment?

Cyber harassment means using the internet or phone to bully, threaten, or harm someone. This includes:

  • Sending threatening or abusive messages
  • Sharing private photos without permission
  • Spreading false information online
  • Stalking someone on social media
  • Blackmail or threats using photos/videos
It is a Crime!

Cyber harassment is punishable under the IT Act 2000 and IPC. You have legal rights. You are NOT alone.

What to Do If It Happens to You

1
Don't Panic — Take Screenshots

Take screenshots of all threatening messages, posts, or calls as evidence.

2
Block the Person

Block them on WhatsApp, Facebook, and all other platforms immediately.

3
Report on the Platform

Use the "Report" button on WhatsApp/Facebook. Platforms take these seriously.

4
File a Cyber Crime Complaint

Call 1930 or visit cybercrime.gov.in to report online. You can also go to your nearest police station.

5
Tell Someone You Trust

Talk to a trusted family member, SHG leader, or NGO worker. Do not suffer in silence.

Know Your Rights

Online Crimes Against Women — What to Do & Where to Report

If any of these happen to you, you are NOT alone and it is NOT your fault. Every situation below is a crime under Indian law. Here is exactly what to do and where to file your complaint.

AI-Generated & Morphed Photos / Deepfakes

Someone used your real photo or video to create a fake, inappropriate, or misleading image using AI or photo editing tools

IT Act Sec.66E & Sec.67
What This Means

Using AI tools (like FaceSwap, DeepFaceLab, or online apps) to put your face on another person's body, create fake explicit images, or make you appear to say something you never said is called a Deepfake. These images are then spread to:

  • Destroy your reputation in your family, village, or workplace
  • Blackmail you into paying money or doing things against your will
  • Harass or humiliate you publicly on social media or WhatsApp groups
Laws that protect you:
  • IT Act Section 66E — violation of privacy (3 years jail + ₹2 lakh fine)
  • IT Act Section 67 — publishing obscene content (5 years jail)
  • IT Act Section 67A — publishing sexually explicit content (7 years jail)
  • BNS Section 79 — voyeurism
Immediate Steps
1

Take screenshots of the fake image, the account that posted it, the post URL, and the date/time.

2

Do NOT share the fake image further — even to show others. Sharing increases its spread.

3

Report on the platform — click the three dots on the post → Report → Nudity/Sexual Content or Harassment.

4

File a complaint online at cybercrime.gov.in under "Report Women/Child Related Crime."

5

Call 1930 — explain it is a Deepfake / morphed photo case. They will guide you through the process.

Private Photo or Video Leak (Non-Consensual)

Someone shared your private, intimate, or personal photos/videos without your permission — also called "Revenge Porn"

IT Act Sec.67A & BNS Sec.79
What This Means

This happens when someone who had your trust — an ex-partner, relative, or person who hacked your phone — shares your private photos or videos publicly to humiliate, control, or blackmail you. This is one of the most serious cybercrimes against women in India.

  • Sharing private images on WhatsApp groups, Facebook, or porn websites
  • Using private photos to demand money, sexual favours, or silence
  • Recording someone without their knowledge during private moments
Laws that protect you:
  • IT Act Section 67A — 7 years imprisonment + fine
  • BNS Section 79 — voyeurism (7 years imprisonment)
  • BNS Section 74 — sexual harassment
  • Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act (if perpetrator is a family member)
Immediate Steps
1

Do NOT pay any blackmail money — paying does not stop the criminal, it encourages more demands.

2

Screenshot everything — the messages, post URLs, account profiles, and date/time as evidence.

3

Request removal from the platform immediately — most platforms remove such content within 24 hours when reported as non-consensual intimate imagery.

4

File a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in immediately. Select "Women/Child Related Crime → Sexually Explicit Content."

5

Contact iCall (9152987821) for free emotional support — this is extremely traumatic and you deserve help.

Data Leak & Privacy Breach

Your personal information — Aadhaar, phone number, photos, bank details — has been stolen or leaked without your knowledge

IT Act Sec.43A & Sec.72
What This Means

A data leak happens when your personal information is accessed, stolen, or made public without your permission. This can happen through:

  • An app or website you used getting hacked
  • Someone installing spyware on your phone
  • You accidentally filling a fake form that collected your data
  • Your Aadhaar or bank details being sold by a fraudulent agent
  • Your phone being accessed by someone in your household without permission
Laws that protect you:
  • IT Act Section 43A — data protection failure by companies (compensation)
  • IT Act Section 72 — breach of confidentiality (2 years + ₹1 lakh fine)
  • Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 — your right to data privacy
Immediate Steps
1

Change all passwords immediately — especially banking, Gmail, and WhatsApp. Use a strong password you've never used before.

2

Check your Aadhaar usage history at myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in — go to "Aadhaar Authentication History" to see if it was used somewhere without your knowledge.

3

Lock your Aadhaar biometrics at myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in → "Lock/Unlock Biometrics" to prevent misuse.

4

Revoke app permissions — go to Phone Settings → Privacy → Permission Manager and remove suspicious apps.

5

Report to CERT-In (India's cybersecurity agency) if you believe a company's database was hacked and your data was leaked.

Account Hacking (WhatsApp, Facebook, Email, Bank)

Someone has taken over your social media, email, or banking account without your permission

IT Act Sec.66 & Sec.66C
What This Means

Account hacking means someone is accessing your accounts — reading your messages, sending messages in your name, stealing money from your bank, or locking you out of your own account. Common ways this happens:

  • You shared your OTP or password with someone
  • You clicked a fake link that stole your login details
  • You installed a fake app that captured your password
  • A family member accessed your phone and changed your passwords
  • SIM swap — scammer got a duplicate SIM of your number
Laws that protect you:
  • IT Act Section 66 — hacking/unauthorised access (3 years + ₹5 lakh fine)
  • IT Act Section 66C — identity theft (3 years + ₹1 lakh fine)
  • IT Act Section 66D — cheating by impersonation (3 years + ₹1 lakh fine)
Immediate Steps
1

WhatsApp hacked? Re-install WhatsApp, enter your phone number, and enter the OTP sent to your SIM — this immediately kicks out the hacker.

2

Facebook/Instagram hacked? Go to facebook.com/hacked or instagram.com/hacked — use the "My account is compromised" option even if you can't log in.

3

Gmail hacked? Go to accounts.google.com/signin/recovery and follow the account recovery steps using your phone number.

4

Bank account hacked? Call your bank's helpline immediately (number on back of your debit card) and ask to freeze your account. Then call 1930.

5

File a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in with all screenshots, transaction details, and account information as evidence.

Blackmail & Sextortion

Someone is threatening to share your photos, videos, or private information unless you pay money or do something against your will

BNS Sec.308 & IT Act Sec.66E
What This Means

Blackmail happens when someone threatens to harm you — financially, socially, or physically — unless you give them what they want. Sextortion is a specific type where the threat involves private or sexual images. This is one of the fastest-growing cybercrimes targeting Indian women. Common situations:

  • An ex-partner or relative threatening to share your private photos
  • A scammer who got your photos through a fake video call and now demands money
  • Someone who hacked your phone demanding payment to not share your data
  • A fake "army officer" or "NRI" who you trusted asking for money to stay silent
NEVER Pay the Blackmailer

Paying does NOT stop the threats — it proves you will pay and the demands will increase. The only way to stop them is to report to police. They cannot actually harm you if you report first.

Laws that protect you:
  • BNS Section 308 — extortion (10 years imprisonment)
  • IT Act Section 66E — privacy violation (3 years + fine)
  • IT Act Section 67 — obscene material (5 years + fine)
  • BNS Section 351 — criminal intimidation (7 years)
Immediate Steps
1

Do NOT pay any money. Do NOT send more photos. Do NOT comply with demands. Paying makes it worse, not better.

2

Stop all contact with the blackmailer immediately — do not respond to threats, ignore messages.

3

Preserve evidence — take screenshots of all threatening messages, calls logs, and any payments made.

4

Report to police immediately — call 1930 or visit your nearest police station. Blackmail cases are taken very seriously by cyber cells.

5

Tell a trusted person — a family member, SHG leader, or NGO. You do not have to face this alone and reporting does not have to be shameful — the criminal is the one who should be ashamed.

Online Stalking & Cyber Harassment

Someone is repeatedly following you online, monitoring your activity, sending unwanted messages, or making you feel unsafe and watched

BNS Sec.78 & IT Act Sec.354D
What This Means

Cyber stalking means someone is following, monitoring, or harassing you repeatedly through the internet or phone — making you fear for your safety. This includes:

  • Sending repeated unwanted messages, friend requests, or calls
  • Monitoring your posts, location, or activity obsessively
  • Creating fake profiles to watch you after being blocked
  • Threatening to harm you or your family online
  • Tracking your location using shared apps or spyware
  • Commenting on every post or tagging you without permission
Laws that protect you:
  • BNS Section 78 — stalking (3 years first offence, 5 years repeat)
  • IT Act Section 354D — cyber stalking (3 years + fine)
  • IT Act Section 66A (for abusive/threatening messages)
Immediate Steps
1

Block on all platforms — WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram, phone. Blocking is not enough alone, but it is the first step.

2

Make all accounts private — so the stalker cannot see your posts or location even from new fake accounts.

3

Keep a record — save all messages, screenshots, dates, and account names. This is your evidence.

4

Check for spyware — go to Phone Settings → Apps and look for apps you don't recognise. Uninstall anything suspicious.

5

File an FIR at your nearest police station or file online at cybercrime.gov.in. Include all screenshots as evidence. Police can trace the stalker even if they are using a fake name.

Online Financial Fraud & UPI Scam

Money has been fraudulently transferred from your bank account, UPI, or you were cheated into sending money to a scammer

IT Act Sec.66D & BNS Sec.318
What This Means

Online financial fraud includes any situation where money was taken from you through digital deception. The most common types targeting SHG women:

  • Approving a "collect request" thinking it was incoming money
  • Sharing OTP with a fake "bank officer" on the phone
  • Installing AnyDesk/TeamViewer that let scammer see your UPI PIN
  • Paying for products on Facebook/Instagram that never arrived
  • SIM swap — scammer received your OTPs and cleared your account
  • Sending money to a romance scammer pretending to be a trusted friend
24-Hour Golden Window

If you report within 24 hours of the fraud, police can freeze the scammer's account before they withdraw the money. After 48 hours, recovery becomes extremely difficult. Call 1930 RIGHT NOW if you were just scammed.

Laws that protect you:
  • IT Act Section 66D — cheating by impersonation (3 years + ₹1 lakh)
  • BNS Section 318 — cheating and dishonestly inducing delivery of property (7 years)
  • BNS Section 316 — criminal breach of trust
Immediate Steps
1

Call 1930 IMMEDIATELY — the National Cyber Crime helpline. This is the fastest way to trigger an account freeze on the scammer's bank account.

2

Call your bank's helpline (number on back of debit card) and ask them to freeze/flag the transaction. Note the complaint reference number.

3

File at cybercrime.gov.in immediately — select "Financial Fraud" and fill in transaction ID, recipient UPI ID/account number, date, and amount.

4

File a complaint with your bank in writing at the branch and ask for a written acknowledgment — you need this to escalate to the Banking Ombudsman if the bank is unresponsive.

5

Escalate to RBI Banking Ombudsman at cms.rbi.org.in if the bank does not resolve your complaint within 30 days.

Fake Identity & Impersonation

Someone created a fake social media profile using your name and photos, or is pretending to be you online to harm your reputation

IT Act Sec.66D & BNS Sec.318
What This Means

Online impersonation means someone is using your identity — your name, your photos, your personal details — to pretend to be you online. This is used to:

  • Borrow money from your contacts pretending to be you
  • Damage your reputation by posting offensive content "as you"
  • Trick people in your network for personal gain
  • Harass your family members through a fake "you"
  • Set up fake profiles to approach men or women in your name
Laws that protect you:
  • IT Act Section 66D — punishment for cheating by personation (3 years + ₹1 lakh)
  • BNS Section 318 — cheating (7 years + fine)
  • BNS Section 356 — defamation (2 years + fine)
Immediate Steps
1

Screenshot the fake profile — capture the profile photo, URL, name, and any posts made from it.

2

Report directly on the platform — go to the fake profile → tap three dots → Report → "Pretending to be me" or "Fake Account."

3

Alert your contacts — post on your real account warning family and friends that a fake profile exists and not to send money or respond to it.

4

File at cybercrime.gov.in — select "Online and Social Media Related Crimes → Fake/Impersonating Profile."

5

File with NCW — if the impersonation is being used for gender-based harassment, the National Commission for Women has a separate complaint portal.
